Assessment Option A • Fluid Dynamics

Charles River Oil Spill Simulation

Applying Continuity, Bernoulli's Principle, Reynolds Number, and Archimedes' Buoyancy to an Environmental Disaster.

100 Total Points

At 12:15 AM, a night crew at a fuel distribution terminal near Cambridge, MA notices a diesel oil release into the Charles River. Environmental engineers estimate that approximately 1,000 gallons ($\approx 3.785 \text{ m}^3$) of diesel fuel has spilled into the river.

The Charles River in Cambridge is approximately 50 meters wide ($W_1 = 50.0 \text{ m}$) with an average depth of 5.0 meters ($h = 5.0 \text{ m}$). It maintains an average volumetric flow rate of 300 ft³/s ($Q \approx 8.50 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$). As the river flows downstream into Boston (a distance of $5.0 \text{ km}$ from the spill point), the channel narrows to a width of 25 meters ($W_2 = 25.0 \text{ m}$), while maintaining the same average depth of $5.0 \text{ m}$. The river flows through Boston for an additional 5.0 km before discharging into Massachusetts Bay.

Schematic View: Charles River Geometry & Spill Velocity Profile

Spill Volume: 1,000 gal ($\approx 3.785 \text{ m}^3$)
Diesel Density ($\rho_{\text{oil}}$): $850 \text{ kg/m}^3$
Water Density ($\rho_{\text{water}}$): $1,000 \text{ kg/m}^3$
Water Viscosity ($\mu$): $1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
Volumetric Flow ($Q$): $8.50 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ ($300 \text{ ft}^3/\text{s}$)
River Depth ($h$): $5.0 \text{ m}$ (constant)
Width 1 (Cambridge): $50.0 \text{ m}$
Width 2 (Boston): $25.0 \text{ m}$

1 Flow Velocity Calculations

15 Points

Calculate the average flow velocity of the Charles River in both the Cambridge section ($v_1$) and the narrowed Boston section ($v_2$). Show all unit conversions and formulas used.

2 Continuity vs. Bernoulli Analysis

10 Points

(a) Explain, using the Equation of Continuity ($A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$), why the river speed increases as it flows into the Boston channel. (5 pts)

(b) Explain, using Bernoulli’s Principle ($P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}$), how the internal dynamic hydrostatic pressure of the fluid changes in the narrowed Boston section as a result of this velocity change. (5 pts)

3 Reynolds Number & Dispersion Behavior

20 Points

Calculate the Reynolds Number ($Re = \frac{\rho v D}{\mu}$) for both river sections using the density and viscosity of water, treating the river depth ($D = 5.0 \text{ m}$) as the characteristic hydraulic dimension. Based on your calculated Reynolds numbers, characterize the nature of the fluid flow (laminar vs. turbulent) and discuss how this will impact the mechanical dispersion and mixing of the oil slick.

4 Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle

10 Points

Using principles of buoyancy and Archimedes’ Principle, explain why diesel oil remains floating on top of the water rather than sinking. Furthermore, calculate the exact percentage (%) of the floating oil layer’s thickness that sits submerged below the water line based on the densities of diesel ($850 \text{ kg/m}^3$) and fresh water ($1,000 \text{ kg/m}^3$).

5 Emergency Response Travel Time

15 Points

Calculate the total time required for the leading edge of the oil spill to travel from the spill site in Cambridge through Boston into Massachusetts Bay ($5.0 \text{ km}$ in Section 1 at velocity $v_1$, plus $5.0 \text{ km}$ in Section 2 at velocity $v_2$). Express your final answer in hours. How much response time does the emergency containment team have before the oil reaches the bay?

6 Environmental & Mitigation Synthesis Essay

30 Points

Write a short, structured synthesis essay (~200–250 words) addressing the following key prompt components:

  • Environmental Impact (10 pts): Effects on aquatic ecosystems, avian wildlife, and dissolved oxygen levels.
  • Social & Economic Impact (10 pts): Consequences for local drinking water intakes, recreational river activities, and public health.
  • Physical Containment & Cleanup (10 pts): Physical containment techniques (e.g., floating booms, skimmers, sorbent pads) and chemical dispersants.
Assessment Option B • Fluid Statics

UK Railway Subsidence & Groundwater Dynamics

Analyzing Hydrostatic Pressure, Soil Moisture Deficit, Archimedes' Buoyancy Loss, and Pascal's Principle.

100 Total Points

During the prolonged summer heatwave and drought in southern England, passenger trains near Lewes (East Sussex) experienced track misalignment and derailments. Geotechnical investigations revealed that intense soil moisture deficit caused groundwater levels within soft clay and peat embankments to plummet.

In normal conditions, saturated embankment soil provides significant upward hydrostatic pore pressure and Archimedes’ buoyant force ($\rho_{\text{water}} g V_{\text{sub}}$) that helps support heavy concrete sub-ballast foundation structures. When severe drought lowers the water table, this buoyant lift is lost, transferring immense additional effective weight onto dry, shrinking subsoil beds, triggering structural settlement and track shifting.

Schematic Cross-Section: Railway Trackbed Groundwater Table Shift

Water Density ($\rho_{\text{water}}$): $1,000 \text{ kg/m}^3$
Concrete Density ($\rho_{\text{concrete}}$): $2,400 \text{ kg/m}^3$
Slurry Density ($\rho_{\text{slurry}}$): $1,200 \text{ kg/m}^3$
Gravity ($g$): $9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$
Atmospheric Pressure ($P_0$): $1.013 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}$
Block Length ($L$): $10.0 \text{ m}$
Block Width ($W$): $3.00 \text{ m}$
Block Height ($H$): $1.00 \text{ m}$

1 Hydrostatic Groundwater Pressure

15 Points

A geotechnical pressure monitoring sensor sits embedded $2.50 \text{ m}$ below the normal groundwater table within the saturated embankment foundation.

(a) Calculate the hydrostatic gauge pressure ($P_{\text{gauge}}$) exerted by the groundwater at this depth. (8 pts)

(b) Calculate the absolute pressure ($P_{\text{abs}}$) at this sensor depth in both Pascals ($\text{Pa}$) and kilopascals ($\text{kPa}$). (7 pts)

2 Normal Conditions: Submerged Buoyancy Lift

15 Points

Prior to the drought, the groundwater table aligns with the top of the concrete sub-ballast foundation block ($10.0 \text{ m} \times 3.00 \text{ m} \times 1.00 \text{ m}$), fully submerging its $30.0 \text{ m}^3$ volume.

(a) Calculate the total gravitational weight force ($W$) of the concrete block in Newtons. (5 pts)

(b) Using Archimedes’ Principle, calculate the upward buoyant force ($F_B$) exerted by the surrounding groundwater. (5 pts)

(c) Calculate the net downward force ($F_{\text{net}}$) that the underlying soil bed must support under these normal conditions. (5 pts)

3 Drought Impact: Loss of Buoyancy & Load Increase

20 Points

During the peak of the drought, the groundwater level drops by 0.80 meters, leaving only 0.20 meters of the foundation block’s height submerged.

(a) Calculate the new reduced buoyant force ($F_{B, \text{drought}}$) acting on the partially submerged block. (6 pts)

(b) Calculate the new net downward force ($F_{\text{net}, \text{drought}}$) exerted onto the drying subsoil bed. (6 pts)

(c) Calculate the percentage increase (%) in downward load borne by the soil compared to normal conditions. Explain how this sudden load transfer causes soil compaction and track displacement. (8 pts)

4 Hydraulic Track Realignment (Pascal's Principle)

10 Points

Railway repair crews utilize hydraulic jacks operating under Pascal’s Principle to lift and re-level settled trackbeds. The hydraulic system consists of a small input cylinder with cross-sectional area $A_1 = 0.0050 \text{ m}^2$ connected to a large lifting cylinder with area $A_2 = 0.500 \text{ m}^2$. If an input force of $F_1 = 1,500 \text{ N}$ is applied to the small piston, calculate the maximum output lifting force ($F_2$) transmitted to the track bed.

5 Apparent Weight in Clay Slurry

10 Points

During repair operations, a reinforced concrete sleeper (tie) with a mass of $300 \text{ kg}$ and volume of $0.150 \text{ m}^3$ drops into an embankment drainage ditch filled with dense clay mud slurry ($\rho_{\text{slurry}} = 1,200 \text{ kg/m}^3$). Calculate the apparent weight ($W_{\text{app}}$) of the concrete sleeper while fully submerged in the slurry.

6 Geotechnical Infrastructure Synthesis Essay

30 Points

Write a short, structured synthesis essay (~200–250 words) analyzing the climate resilience of transport infrastructure built on soft clay soils:

  • Physical Mechanics (15 pts): Analyze how extreme drought-to-deluge weather cycles destabilize railway trackbeds through changes in groundwater pore pressure, loss of buoyant lift, and soil volumetric shrinkage.
  • Geotechnical Solutions (15 pts): Propose and evaluate two civil engineering mitigation strategies (e.g., deep pile foundations, continuous soil moisture monitoring networks, controlled subgrade irrigation) to prevent future derailments.

Physics 2 Honors — Fluid Mechanics Formula Sheet

Official Reference Equations & Physical Constants

Fluid Statics

Density: $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$

Fluid Pressure: $P = \frac{F}{A}$

Gauge Hydrostatic Pressure: $P_{\text{gauge}} = \rho g h$

Absolute Hydrostatic Pressure: $P_{\text{abs}} = P_0 + \rho g h$

Archimedes' Buoyant Force: $F_B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{sub}} g$

Pascal's Principle: $\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}$

Fluid Dynamics

Volumetric Flow Rate: $Q = A v = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t}$

Equation of Continuity: $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$

Bernoulli's Equation: $P_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 + \rho g h_1 = \text{constant}$

Reynolds Number: $Re = \frac{\rho v D}{\mu}$

Fraction Submerged (Floating): $\text{Fraction} = \frac{\rho_{\text{object}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}$
